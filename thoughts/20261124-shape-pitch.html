Field Note · Shaping Before Planning

Plans assume you're building. Pitches test if you should.

A coding harness's plan mode decomposes files, schemas, and steps. By the time that plan exists, sunk effort has already built a quiet bias toward doing the work. /shape-pitch keeps an idea loose and abstract long enough to judge what it is worth—or walk away.

01 / Altitude & Bias

Why not just use the harness's plan mode?

Every modern coding harness ships with a plan mode. Hand it a rough idea or a back-end grab-bag, and it behaves like an eager contractor who assumes the bet is already signed: within thirty seconds it inventories directories, names files to edit, and drafts schemas or endpoint signatures.

Premature concreteness is the trap. Once an implementation plan looks detailed—and you have spent twenty minutes reading and nudging it—you develop a quiet, subjective bias toward doing rather than not doing, before ever asking whether the problem is worth two weeks or two hours.

Ryan Singer’s Shape Up puts a deliberate step ahead of planning. A pitch keeps an idea rough, solved, and bounded—loose and abstract enough to weigh the appetite, surface rabbit holes, and drop weak bets cheaply before anyone writes a spec. /shape-pitch turns that discipline into a reusable agent skill.

02 / Non-UI Shaping

No screen to sketch? Draw the caller and the invariants.

Left to a generic prompt, asking a model for a “Shape Up pitch” produces a polite template: it invents a two-week estimate, restates your feature request under Problem, and pads out Rabbit holes with boilerplate. Worse, most of the work I shape—libraries, APIs, grid-data pipelines, and domain models—has no UI screen to fat-marker sketch.

When I built /shape-pitch and /pitch-add for Pi (my-pi-config@1cb9d66), the goal was to encode pushback and altitude rather than headings:

1. Two things the agent may never invent. It reads the source material first, then stops and asks—one question at a time—for the appetite and one specific failing story if either is missing. Grab-bags (“refactor reference data”, “pipeline v2”) get pushed back until restated as one caller’s problem.

2. Non-UI breadboards and fat-marker call sites. Where a UI pitch names screens and buttons, references/non-ui.md translates them to boundaries, operations, and data flow, five lines of caller pseudocode, and contracts & invariants—stopping well short of SQL migrations or OpenAPI schemas.

Side note · What is a breadboard?

Borrowed from electrical engineering via Shape Up: a sketch that shows what connects to what without deciding what it looks like—all the components and wiring, zero industrial design. For a UI, you write only words and arrows: places (screens or dialogs), affordances (buttons, fields, copy), and connections (what leads where), keeping pixel debates out of shaping. For back-end work, “what it looks like” means schemas and type signatures—so a non-UI breadboard maps boundaries, operations, and data flow instead (as in Figure 2 below).

You can judge in two minutes whether this fits a two-week batch—or decide ComEd alone isn't worth two weeks right now—while leaving the team room to design the actual tables and module layout.

03 / Pi to Antigravity

Author the judgment once. Swap the thin harness seam.

I wrote /shape-pitch and /pitch-add inside my Pi configuration first. The next morning, rather than rewriting the workflow for Antigravity, I pointed Breeze (my persistent Orca coordinator bot) at scaryPonens/my-pi-config and asked it to adapt the skill.

Because all of the domain thinking lived in plain Markdown reference files, ninety percent of the skill carried over untouched. Only the thin outer seam—how slash commands enter, how structured questions prompt, and where files save—needed translation. We then immediately used the ported skill three times in a row to shape the site rebuild, the Orca with bots note, and this piece into Obsidian.

04 / Where It Still Bites

Three failure boundaries from porting and dogfooding it.

Encoding a shaping practice into a skill sharpens the conversation, but every cross-hatched box at the bottom of Figure 2 showed up in real use today:

1. Stale concepts port just as faithfully as good judgment. Because Breeze copied the core rules straight across from my-pi-config, an older framing I am archiving rode along into Antigravity untouched—and immediately surfaced in the multiple-choice prompt while shaping this very page. Portable skills need pruning in one canonical place, or old vocabulary echoes across every harness.

2. Codebases pull hard toward plan-mode detail. Models have seen millions of SQL schemas, OpenAPI specs, and TypeScript interfaces. The moment you feed a real repository or an old HLD into /pitch-add, gravity tugs the draft down toward implementation detail unless the invariant ceiling in non-ui.md holds it back.

3. Pushback enforces sequence, not the bet. /shape-pitch stops an agent from inventing an appetite, smoothing over a contradiction between two docs, or jumping into file edits prematurely. Deciding whether an idea is worth two weeks, one evening, or walking away is still yours alone.

05 / Check Yourself

Pitch, source fold, or implementation plan?

Pick the tool that matches the stage of thinking on each scenario below.

1. Someone suggests “cleaning up how utility tariff rules load,” and nobody has said what broke today or whether fixing it is worth two days or six weeks.
2. Halfway through shaping a pitch in Obsidian, you find an old RFC claiming snapshots are rewritten in place while HEAD translates them on read.
3. The pitch is already bet on: appetite locked at two weeks, invariants agreed, rabbit holes patched, and no-gos explicit. Now you need exact file edits and test runs.
